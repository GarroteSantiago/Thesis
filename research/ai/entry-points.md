# Entry points

Peffers et al. define four entry points [1]. The DSRM sequence is the same in all four; only the starting activity changes. The `thesis` skill asks for one (`skills.md`).

| Entry point [1] | Starts at | Trigger | Route |
|---|---|---|---|
| Problem-centred initiation | DSRM 1 | An observed problem | Step 0 (`0-start/`: the observation) → SLS 1–5 → DSRM 1 built from the observation and the SLS → 2 → 3–5 loop → 6. **This thesis.** `flow-sls.md` and `flow-dsrm.md` are this route. |
| Objective-centred solution | DSRM 2 | A need that a better artifact could meet | Step 0 (`0-start/`: my draft objectives) → SLS 1–5 → DSRM 1 written, 2 revised → 3–5 loop → 6 |
| Design and development-centred initiation | DSRM 3 | An existing artifact not yet thought through as a solution to this problem | Step 0 (`0-start/`: the artifact, described) → SLS 1–5 → DSRM 1–2 written for the problem it solves → 3–5 loop → 6 |
| Client/context initiated | DSRM 4 | A practical solution observed to work | Step 0 (`0-start/`: the observed solution, described) → SLS 1–5 → work backward through DSRM 3, 2, 1 to apply rigor retroactively [1] → 5 → 6 |

My rules for all four (not part of Peffers):
- Step 0 is always my input in `0-start/`, frozen before the SLS starts (`kickstart.md`). What it holds depends on the entry point.
- The SLS always runs right after step 0, because DSRM 1–3 need it (`research/README.md`).
- The kickstart safeguards apply whatever the entry point.
