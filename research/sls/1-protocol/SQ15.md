# SQ15

## Question
Which system functions (resource allocation, scheduling, naming, protection, failure handling, global consistency) have been shown to require global agreement or to work better with central or logically central coordination, and what do decentralised versions cost or give up?

## Why it matters to the observation
The notes want to remove the middle layer entirely. Agreement among message-passing parts has proven limits (Fischer, Lynch and Paterson, 1985, *Impossibility of Distributed Consensus with One Faulty Process*, JACM), and networking, the notes' own model, moved towards logically centralised control with software-defined networking (Kreutz et al., 2015, Proceedings of the IEEE). If key operating system functions need a coordinator, "no central coordinator" is a constraint that may make RQ5's answer "no" by construction.
