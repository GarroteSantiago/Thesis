# SQ8

## Question
What empirical evidence shows that the hardware's execution model, rather than the language, libraries, tools or training, determines how programmers design and write programs, and how correct those programs are?

## Why it matters to the observation
The notes' first premise ("the machine model shapes how programs are written") rests only on two Turing Award lectures, which are essays, not measurements. If the evidence says the language or the runtime decides how people write programs, then changing the hardware does not change the programs on top of it, and the motivation needs another basis.

## Origin
counter-evidence (added by skeptic)

## Kind
evidence, because the question asks what the evidence says about whether the hardware model or the language side shapes programs: findings (measurements, claims, counter-arguments). (`research/sls/1-protocol/step.md`, *How much each question needs*)

## Unit
A finding: a measurement or argument that the hardware's execution model, or the language, libraries, tools or training, shapes how programs are designed or how correct they are.

## Enough when
At least 10 papers serving it are read, in the reading order, then 3 in a row add nothing new.

## Reading order
Measurements before essays, hardware side and language side alternating: the large studies of language and defects (P147, P148) alternating with the arguments that hardware is shaped by, or shapes, the language's machine model (P4, P2), then the language-side essay (P3) and Ditzel and Patterson's retrospective (P280). Round 0 holds only these 6 serving papers, fewer than the 10 the rule needs: all are read, and the question stays open until later rounds bring more.
