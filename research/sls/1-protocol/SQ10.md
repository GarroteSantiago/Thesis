# SQ10

## Question
What does the literature report about the equivalence (duality) of message-passing and shared-memory system structures, and can either one be built on top of the other while keeping its properties (encapsulation, freedom from data races, interposition) and its performance?

## Why it matters to the observation
Lauer and Needham (1978/1979, *On the Duality of Operating System Structures*) argue that message-oriented and procedure-oriented systems are duals: a problem solved in one has a dual solution in the other. If the two are equivalent, the observation's gap may be a matter of which layer exposes which model, and putting messages in hardware may add nothing that a layer above cannot give.

## Origin
counter-evidence (added by skeptic)

## Kind
evidence, because the question asks what the literature reports about equivalence and about building one model on the other: findings (proofs, arguments, measurements), not a list of systems. (`research/sls/1-protocol/step.md`, *How much each question needs*)

## Unit
A finding: a result or argument on the equivalence of the two structures, or a report of one model built on the other, with which properties (encapsulation, freedom from data races, interposition) and how much performance it keeps or loses.

## Enough when
At least 10 papers serving it are read, in the reading order, then 5 in a row add nothing new. 10 + 5 rather than 10 + 3: the evidence comes from unrelated fields (theory of distributed computing, operating systems, distributed shared memory, multiprocessor hardware, parallel programming measurements).

## Reading order
Theory first, then the two directions alternating, measurements before designs. Lauer and Needham's duality (P18), the partial equivalence under failures (P86); then shared memory built on messages (P279, P269, P271, P275, P267) alternating with messages built on, or beside, shared memory (P274 Alewife, P50 Determinator, P268, P270, P66), the measured comparisons of pure message passing against hybrid models (P90, P219), two views of concurrency in object systems (P255), StarfishOS (P191), COMMOS (P253), Lisp (P15). Round 0 holds 18 papers that serve it (counted as the included rows whose screening reason names this question, plus P275 and P279, whose reasons say only "network" or "library" but whose titles are about shared memory over message passing).
