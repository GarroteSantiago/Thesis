# SQ11

## Question
Why do developers of actor or message-passing programs also use shared state, locks or other concurrency mechanisms alongside messages, and what do they report that the pure message model cannot express or costs too much?

## Why it matters to the observation
The observation wants a system with no escape routes from its own rules. Tasharofi, Dinges and Johnson (2013, *Why Do Scala Developers Mix the Actor Model with other Concurrency Models?*, ECOOP) report that most of the large actor programs they studied mix actors with other concurrency models, partly because of shortcomings of the actor model itself. If programmers keep building escape routes where they have the choice, a machine that removes them may be unusable rather than cleaner.

## Origin
counter-evidence (added by skeptic)

## Kind
evidence, because the question asks why developers mix models and what they report the pure model cannot do: findings (reasons, limits, costs). (`research/sls/1-protocol/step.md`, *How much each question needs*)

## Unit
A finding: a reason developers give or a study reports for using shared state, locks or other mechanisms alongside messages, or a thing the pure message model is reported unable to express or too costly for.

## Enough when
At least 10 papers serving it are read, in the reading order, then 3 in a row add nothing new.

## Reading order
Empirical studies of real programs first (P19), then designs that add shared state to actors, each stating what the pure model lacked (P93, P92), transactional memory in actors (P55, P56), the type system for race-free messages on shared-memory runtimes (P254), and the L4 lessons (P48). Round 0 holds only these 7 serving papers, fewer than the 10 the rule needs: all are read, and the question stays open until later rounds bring more.
