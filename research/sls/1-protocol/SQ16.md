# SQ16

## Question
What do studies of reflective and metacircular systems report about whether a system can be defined entirely in its own terms, what must remain outside it as a base (primitives, a level that is not reflected), and what that base costs or prevents?

## Why it matters to the observation
The observation says a system with escape routes "cannot define itself" and "cannot be taken seriously". Smith's reflective tower (1984, *Reflection and Semantics in Lisp*, POPL) is, at least virtually, an infinite stack of levels, and Squeak's virtual machine is written in Smalltalk but translated to C to run (Ingalls et al., 1997, OOPSLA). If every self-defining system needs a base that is not defined in itself, the gap may be unavoidable and the goal has to be restated as moving the base, not removing it.
