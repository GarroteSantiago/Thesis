# SQ18

## Question
What costs of object-based and capability-based hardware do independent evaluations attribute to object orientation, fine-grained protection or object addressing themselves, rather than to compilers or implementation, and how large are those costs?

## Why it matters to the observation
H2 (the 432's losses were avoidable) rests on one paper by the 432's own evaluators, and the same paper still names a residual 1–4 times slowdown as the inherent cost of the 432's style [notes, citing Colwell et al. 1988, pp. 336–337]. An earlier independent evaluation (Hansen et al., 1982, *A Performance Evaluation of the Intel iAPX 432*) and modern capability hardware (Woodruff et al., 2014, *The CHERI Capability Model*, ISCA) measure the cost from other angles. H2 holds only if the inherent part is small across sources, not in one.

## Origin
counter-evidence (added by skeptic)

## Kind
evidence, because the question asks what costs evaluations attribute to object orientation, fine-grained protection or object addressing, and how large they are: findings (measured costs and their attribution). (`research/sls/1-protocol/step.md`, *How much each question needs*)

## Unit
A finding: a cost of object-based or capability-based hardware, its size, and whether the evaluation attributes it to object orientation, fine-grained protection or object addressing themselves or to compilers or implementation, with whether the evaluator is independent of the designers.

## Enough when
At least 10 papers serving it are read, in the reading order, then 3 in a row add nothing new.

## Reading order
Independent evaluations first, old and modern hardware alternating: the independent 432 evaluation (P29), CHERI (P30), the 432 evaluators' paper (P10), the low-overhead security-tagged architecture (P144), lightweight protection for small objects (P62), the CHERI virtual prototype (P142), the CHERI instruction-set specification (P143), Myers's book on the 432 and SWARD (P1). Round 0 holds these 8 serving papers, fewer than the 10 the rule needs: all are read, and the question stays open until later rounds bring more.
