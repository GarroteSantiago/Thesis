# SQ14

## Question
What evidence shows that hardware shared memory, cache coherence and shared-memory operating system kernels scale to many cores, and what limits do studies report on the scaling of many-core hardware in general (power, communication, serial fractions)?

## Why it matters to the observation
The notes say shared memory "has become a scaling cost" and that independent actors map onto many cores. Martin, Hill and Sorin (2012, *Why On-Chip Cache Coherence Is Here to Stay*, CACM) argue coherence can scale with bounded cost; Boyd-Wickizer et al. (2010, OSDI) conclude there is no scalability reason yet to give up traditional kernel organisation on 48 cores; Esmaeilzadeh et al. (2011, *Dark Silicon and the End of Multicore Scaling*, ISCA) project limits on multicore speedup itself. Each one weakens a premise of H3 or of the parallel-hardware motivation.
