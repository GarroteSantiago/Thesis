# SQ14

## Question
What evidence shows that hardware shared memory, cache coherence and shared-memory operating system kernels scale to many cores, and what limits do studies report on the scaling of many-core hardware in general (power, communication, serial fractions)?

## Why it matters to the observation
The notes say shared memory "has become a scaling cost" and that independent actors map onto many cores. Martin, Hill and Sorin (2012, *Why On-Chip Cache Coherence Is Here to Stay*, CACM) argue coherence can scale with bounded cost; Boyd-Wickizer et al. (2010, OSDI) conclude there is no scalability reason yet to give up traditional kernel organisation on 48 cores; Esmaeilzadeh et al. (2011, *Dark Silicon and the End of Multicore Scaling*, ISCA) project limits on multicore speedup itself. Each one weakens a premise of H3 or of the parallel-hardware motivation.

## Origin
counter-evidence (added by skeptic)

## Kind
evidence, because the question asks what the evidence shows about scaling and its limits: findings (measurements, projections, arguments), not a list of chips. (`research/sls/1-protocol/step.md`, *How much each question needs*)

## Unit
A finding: a measurement, projection or argument that hardware shared memory, cache coherence or a shared-memory kernel does or does not scale to many cores, or a limit reported on many-core scaling in general (power, communication, serial fractions).

## Enough when
At least 10 papers serving it are read, in the reading order, then 5 in a row add nothing new. 10 + 5 rather than 10 + 3: the question joins three fields (coherence hardware, operating system kernels, power and parallel-speedup limits), and a run of coherence-protocol papers says nothing about kernels or power.

## Reading order
The three fields alternating, and both sides alternating within each: coherence scales (P22) against the case for message passing on many-core chips (P98); Linux scales to 48 cores (P23) against the multikernel and fos (P8, P102); dark silicon (P24) and the Berkeley view (P111) for general limits; then synchronisation measured across many-core platforms (P103), Tornado (P47), EM2 (P108), the many-core kernel executive (P110), kernels on multicore (P252), and only then the coherence-protocol theses and papers (P105, P106, P107, P109), one at a time, since they resemble each other. Round 0 holds 15 papers that serve it (the included rows whose screening reason names this question; P8 is added because it argues the opposite side).
