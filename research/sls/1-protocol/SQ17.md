# SQ17

## Question
Which mechanisms let a layer be put between a program and conventional shared-memory hardware or kernels without changing either (virtual memory, trap-and-emulate virtualisation, system call interposition, input/output memory management), and how do their coverage and cost compare with interposition on messages?

## Why it matters to the observation
The observation says that at the low level "layers cannot be put in between the way the network allows". Virtualisation is such a layer: Popek and Goldberg (1974, *Formal Requirements for Virtualizable Third Generation Architectures*, CACM) give the conditions under which a monitor can intercept every sensitive instruction. SQ3 only looks at message-based systems, so it cannot find this counter-evidence.
