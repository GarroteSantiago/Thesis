# SQ17

## Question
Which mechanisms let a layer be put between a program and conventional shared-memory hardware or kernels without changing either (virtual memory, trap-and-emulate virtualisation, system call interposition, input/output memory management), and how do their coverage and cost compare with interposition on messages?

## Why it matters to the observation
The observation says that at the low level "layers cannot be put in between the way the network allows". Virtualisation is such a layer: Popek and Goldberg (1974, *Formal Requirements for Virtualizable Third Generation Architectures*, CACM) give the conditions under which a monitor can intercept every sensitive instruction. SQ3 only looks at message-based systems, so it cannot find this counter-evidence.

## Origin
counter-evidence (added by skeptic)

## Kind
kinds, because the question names kinds of mechanism (virtual memory, trap-and-emulate virtualisation, system call interposition, input/output memory management) and compares their coverage and cost with interposition on messages; one well-measured example of each kind answers it, while the monitors and hypervisors that use them are far too many to list. (`research/sls/1-protocol/step.md`, *How much each question needs*)

Census or kinds is my decision at gate 1c; kinds is recommended, but in round 0 the two are close. Estimate from `sls/2-search/R0.md`: 15 included papers serve it (P28, P49, P129–P141; 14 name it in their screening reason, and P49 had it added at 3c). Under census: all 15 in round 0, then every new monitor, hypervisor or interposition system each later round finds, in a field with a very large literature. Under kinds: about 13 papers (10 + 3), since round 0 already shows about 6 kinds (trap-and-emulate, paravirtualisation, hardware-assisted virtualisation, system call interposition, device and input/output virtualisation, isolation kernels).

## Unit
A kind of mechanism that puts a layer between a program and conventional shared-memory hardware or kernels without changing either, with its coverage (what it can and cannot intercept) and its cost. A kind already recorded counts as new again if a different coverage limit or cost is reported.

## Enough when
At least 10 papers serving it are read, in the reading order, then 3 in a row add nothing new.

## Reading order
The formal conditions first (P28), then mechanisms alternating and decades alternating, measured comparisons first: software against hardware x86 virtualisation (P138), Xen paravirtualisation (P136), Intel's hardware support (P137), system call interposition (P132, then P135), input/output cost in Xen (P129, P140), device virtualisation in a hosted monitor (P139), input/output security and performance (P133), the Denali isolation kernel (P141), microkernel isolation of drivers (P49), the 1970s monitor measurements (P131, P134), the monitor comparison (P130).
