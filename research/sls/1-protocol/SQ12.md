# SQ12

## Question
Where do measured costs and scaling limits come from in actor and message-passing runtimes on conventional hardware, and do their authors identify the hardware's shared-memory model as the limiting factor, or report software techniques that remove it?

## Why it matters to the observation
The notes say compiler techniques made language-specific processors unnecessary for Smalltalk and Self. If current actor runtimes (measured, for example, with the Savina benchmark suite, Imam and Sarkar 2014, AGERE!) reach their limits for reasons that a message-based machine would not change, the design problem loses its "improve execution" half.

## Origin
counter-evidence (added by skeptic)

## Kind
evidence, because the question asks where measured costs come from and to what their authors attribute them: findings (measurements and attributed causes). (`research/sls/1-protocol/step.md`, *How much each question needs*)

## Unit
A finding: a measured cost or scaling limit of an actor or message-passing runtime on conventional hardware, with the cause its authors give (the hardware's shared-memory model or something else), or a software technique reported to remove it.

## Enough when
At least 10 papers serving it are read, in the reading order, then 3 in a row add nothing new.

## Reading order
Measurement studies first, then software techniques, alternating: the Savina suite (P20), actor runtimes on hierarchical shared-memory multicores (P94), ownership transfer to avoid copying messages (P95), the Scala message-passing runtime (P91), L4 IPC cost (P46). Round 0 holds only these 5 serving papers, fewer than the 10 the rule needs: all are read, and the question stays open until later rounds bring more.
