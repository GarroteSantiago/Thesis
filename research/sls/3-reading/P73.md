## First pass: the five Cs
- **Category:** System reference manual of an implemented system. It "describes both the language and the 'operating system' of the Lisp Machine" (P73, preface, PDF p. 3) and "is the reference manual for the Zetalisp language. This document is not a tutorial" (P73, p. 1).
- **Context:** The Lisp Machine is "a new computer system designed to provide a high performance and economical implementation of the Lisp language", a "personal computation system" (P73, p. 1); Zetalisp is "closely related to the Maclisp dialect" (P73, p. 1). Its object facility follows "the object oriented programming style used in the Smalltalk and Actor families of languages" (P73, p. 279). No reference list (manual).
- **Correctness:** A manual makes no claims to validate. Its stated assumptions: each user gets "your own processor and memory system for the duration of the session" (P73, p. 1), and "Usually only one process at a time is runnable" (P73, p. 430). Its few performance statements are rough estimates: "the actual speed may vary from these estimates by as much as a factor of two" (P73, p. 484). Parts are said to be in flux: "most of what is in" the subprimitives chapter "is considered subject to change without notice" (P73, p. 172); the paging system section reads "[Someday this may discuss how it works.]" (P73, p. 183).
- **Contributions:** Complete documentation of Zetalisp (P73, preface) and of "the stabler parts" of the "operating-system-like parts of the system" (P73, preface); chapter 20 documents flavors, "a language facility to provide generic functions using the paradigm used in Smalltalk and the Actor families of languages, called 'object-oriented programming' or 'message passing'", "widely used by the system programs of the Lisp Machine" (P73, p. 2); chapter 27 documents the instruction set (P73, p. 2).
- **Clarity:** Clear and well indexed (concept, flavor, message, meter indexes; P73, p. ix). It excludes the window system and user interface (P73, preface).

## Notes
- **Source and version read:** the 4th edition (July 1981) of the manual, scanned PDF from Bitsavers (`https://bitsavers.trailing-edge.com/pdf/mit/cadr/chinual_4thEd_Jul81.pdf`), saved as `papers/P73.pdf`. The DOI target is a one-page notice in ACM SIGART Bulletin no. 78 (Crossref: p. 10–10); its PDF at dl.acm.org was not reachable (Cloudflare challenge), so only its Crossref/OpenAlex abstract was read. The notice gives "471-page"; the scan has 532 numbered body pages plus ix front-matter pages, so the notice may describe a different printing (inferred, not checked). The edition is matched to the notice by date (manual July 1981, notice October 1981) and authors; that it is the edition announced is inferred.
- **Criterion:** the full text supports I1(b) at the microcode level: when an instance is called with a message keyword, "The microcode binds self to the object, binds the instance variables ... and calls a dtp-select-method" (p. 309), and the term "message" is used throughout (pp. 283–285).
