## First pass: the five Cs
- **Category:** A tutorial description of one commercial processor architecture designed by others. The abstract says "these are covered in a tutorial fashion" (abstract). It is not an evaluation, as far as the abstract shows.
- **Context:** It is "intended as a companion to a recently published paper (Witten, 1981) which surveys the architectures of the new generation of 16-bit microprocessors, but which was written prior to the announcement of the iAPX 432" (abstract). The material is "related to earlier developments in computer architecture" (abstract). The reference list shows which developments (Crossref metadata, not the paper's pages): B5700/B6700, capability-based addressing (Fabry 1974), the Cambridge CAP (Needham 1977), MU5, Smalltalk (Ingalls 1978; Kaehler 1981), Ada, and Rattner (1981) on the 432.
- **Correctness:** Cannot be judged from the abstract. It makes no performance claim and reports no evidence (abstract).
- **Contributions:** An explanation of the 432's "sophisticated segmentation structure and the design of its instruction set", which the abstract says is where "the novelty of the new processor lies chiefly" (abstract).
- **Clarity:** Cannot be judged; the full text was not read.
