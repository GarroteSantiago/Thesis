## First pass: the five Cs
- **Category:** Retrospective and position paper. It critiques the published justifications ("axioms") for high-level language computers (HLLCs) and proposes a definition, three measures, ten design attributes and a debugging scheme. It reports no measurement of its own (pp. 97–103).
- **Context:** HLLC proposals for ALGOL, APL, BASIC, COBOL, FORTRAN, LISP, PASCAL, PL/I and SNOBOL [1–19]. Only "a handful" were implemented [4, 7, 9, 20, 21] (p. 97). Machines discussed: SYMBOL [20, 31–34, 47], Burroughs B6700 [29], Manchester MU5 [30] and NCR Criterion [9, 40] (pp. 98–100, 102). Chu's definition of an HLLC system [39] (p. 100). McKeeman (1967) [23] (p. 98).
- **Correctness:** Rests on argument and experience, mainly with SYMBOL, plus two figures cited from others: the Interdata 8/32 code-compaction experiment [35] (p. 99) and Shustek's PL/I statement-number overhead [50] (p. 102). The premise is stated as a hedge: "a review of existing justifications reveals many shortcomings" (p. 97). Most claims are hedged ("appear questionable", p. 97; "It is our feeling that", p. 100; "we would like to speculate", p. 101).
- **Contributions:** The paper states them: "to identify and discuss several issues applicable to high-level language computer architecture, to provide a more concrete definition of high-level language computers, and to suggest a direction for high-level language computer architectures of the future" (p. 97). Delivered as: responses to six axioms (pp. 98–100); a three-requirement definition of a High-Level Language Computer System (HLLCS) (p. 100); the measures HLLESF, HLLSSF and HLLPSF (p. 101); ten attributes of a "high-performance cost-effective HLLCS of the mid 1980's" (pp. 101–102); and a decompilation scheme for source-level error reporting (pp. 102–103).
- **Clarity:** Clear and argumentative, with no figures or tables. There is no abstract; the paper opens with an Introduction (p. 97).

## Concepts
| Concept | What the paper says | Page |
|---|---|---|
| High-level language computer (HLLC) | Machines "directed towards the execution of" particular high-level languages; "only a handful ... have actually been implemented". | p. 97 |
| High-Level Language Computer System (HLLCS) | A system that "(1) Uses high-level languages for all programming, debugging and other user/system interactions. (2) Discovers and reports syntax and execution time errors in terms of the high-level language source program. (3) Does not have any outward appearance of transformations from the user programming language to any internal languages". "This definition does not restrict the implementation to hardware or software". | p. 100 |
| Direct execution machine | Machines "defined to execute a source program without any form of translation to an intermediate language". The authors expect them not to "become viable". | p. 100 |
| High-Level Language Execution Support Factor (HLLESF) | "the ratio of T_L to T_H", where T_H is execution time on an HLLCS and T_L is the time on a fast system that is not constrained to check and report errors. It is "an indication of how well the architecture supports a HLLCS". | p. 101 |
| HLL Size Support Factor (HLLSSF), HLL Preparation time Support Factor (HLLPSF) | Ratios of program size and preparation time (compilation, linking, loading) between the same two cases; "less important than HLLESF". | p. 101 |
| Semantic gap | One of the commonly cited justifications: "Reduce the semantic gap between programming and machine languages". The paper lists it without defining it. | p. 97 |
| Transparent reversibility of compilation | Errors are reported at source level from three data structures stored with each load module: an address map, a copy of the source and a symbol table. A decompiled version of the failing statement is then pattern-matched against the original to find the operator or operand at fault. Decompiling the whole program is "not recommended", because "it is not possible to recreate the original source line exactly". Stack machines "offer instruction sets which are easiest to decompile". | pp. 102–103 |
