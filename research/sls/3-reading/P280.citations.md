## Suggested fixes
1. Performance results (HLLESF row), p. 101:
   `SYMBOL "has a HLLESF of one" and the B6700 meets the definition (stated without measurement).`
   →
   `SYMBOL is "an example of a machine with a HLLESF of one"; for the B6700 the claim is conditional: "if the B6700 meets our definition of HLLCS, it also does not gain performance by turning off error checking" (both stated without measurement).`

2. Fate and reasons (direct execution row), p. 100:
   `The reason given is that programs must first be checked for syntax errors, "tantamount to compiling".`
   →
   `The principal reason given is that programs must first be checked "for syntax or detectable semantic errors", steps that "account for most of the phases of a compiler"; and retaining intermediate information to avoid retranslation "is tantamount to compiling".`

3. Hardware–language trade-off (cost row), p. 98:
   `Cost: HLLCs "require more logic for their implementation than their contemporary counterparts" (p. 98).`
   →
   `Cost: the paper names as the "first inference" of Axiom 3 that HLLCs "require more logic for their implementation than their contemporary counterparts", extra hardware that proponents justify with "the cliche of the falling cost of hardware" (p. 98).`

4. Concepts (transparent reversibility row), pp. 102–103:
   `Errors are reported at source level by decompiling the object program, using an address map, a copy of the source and a symbol table.`
   →
   `Errors are reported at source level from three data structures stored with each load module: an address map, a copy of the source and a symbol table. A decompiled version of the failing statement is then pattern-matched against the original to find the operator or operand at fault. Decompiling the whole program is "not recommended", because "it is not possible to recreate the original source line exactly".`
