## First pass: the five Cs
- **Category:** Historical account of a programming language (a HOPL paper), organised as "a series of slices through the subject matter" (p. 2): projects and people, technical themes, Lisp as a language laboratory, and why Lisp evolved as it did. It includes a history of the "ascension and decline of special purpose Lisp machines" (p. 1, abstract).
- **Context:** Picks up "where McCarthy's paper in the first HOPL conference left off" (p. 1, abstract; p. 2). The paper does not state its sources; from the text they are mostly the authors' own participation and the archived E-mail of the Common Lisp effort (pp. 21, 32), plus manuals (inferred). It cites Lisp machine papers and manuals (Deutsch 1973, Greenblatt 1974, Moon 1984, Weinreb and Moon 1978/1981) (pp. 12–13; reference list pp. 69–77).
- **Correctness:** The Lisp machine claims are narrative and qualitative, with no measurement reported; reasons are often hedged ("perhaps", "probably", "possibly") (pp. 15, 27, 28). The authors were participants: both in the Common Lisp Group (p. 21), and Gabriel a founder of Lucid, which became "the primary stock-hardware Lisp company" (pp. 30–31), i.e. a competitor of the Lisp machine companies (inferred). The manuscript read has unresolved citations shown as "[?]" (e.g. p. 13, p. 27).
- **Contributions:** A chronology of Lisp dialects and implementations from Lisp 1.5 to the ANSI and ISO standards; the technical evolution of selected features (NIL and T, iteration, macros, numerical facilities, notable failures); Lisp as a language laboratory; "some reflections on the forces that have driven the evolution of Lisp" (p. 1, abstract).
- **Clarity:** Clearly written and well organised; the authors warn the slice-by-slice organisation "inevitably leads to some redundancy" (p. 2). Lisp machine material is spread over several sections (2.5, 2.6, 2.9, 2.10, 2.11.2, 2.12).
