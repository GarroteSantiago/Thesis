## First pass: the five Cs
- **Category:** Descriptive book chapter on one architecture, followed by a short evaluative discussion of it (pp. 159–186; "9.8 Discussion", pp. 184–186).
- **Context:** One of a series of chapters on capability-based systems; compares the 432 with the IBM System/38 (p. 159), Carnegie-Mellon's Hydra (p. 159, p. 167), STAROS (p. 185) and the Dennis and Van Horn model (p. 185). Describes the 432 "as of revision 3 [Intel 82]" (p. 160).
- **Correctness:** Based on Intel's manuals and Organick (1983), cited (p. 159, p. 186). The one performance figure (CALL time, p. 185) and the installed-base figure (p. 186) have no stated source or method.
- **Contributions:** A structured account of the 432's object addressing, program execution, type managers, storage resources and instruction encoding (pp. 161–184), and a third-party assessment of its strengths, limits and commercial fate (pp. 184–186). Interprocess communication by messages to ports is mentioned only briefly (p. 159, p. 162, p. 171).
- **Clarity:** Clear and well illustrated (15 figures, 3 tables); the scan's OCR text has errors but the page images are legible.

## Notes
- Source: the full text, the author-hosted scan of the 1984 printed chapter (https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~levy/capabook/Chapter9.pdf, OCR by Acrobat Capture, PDF created 2002), downloaded to `papers/P72.pdf`. The printed pages are 159–186, matching the Crossref range for the DOI. Read in full, to pass 2.
- Criterion, I1: met. The 432 has instructions "that send messages to and receive messages from port objects" (p. 171), interprocess communication implemented "in hardware and microcode" (p. 159), and a MESSAGE PORT system object (p. 162). This meets I1(a)/(b) with the terms "message" and "interprocess communication". The chapter gives little detail of the message mechanism, which it defers to Cox et al. (1983) (p. 186).
- Criterion, I2 (proposed in addition; the verdict is the gate's): the chapter describes "the first object-based microprocessor" (p. 159), gives a CALL timing (p. 185), and states reasons for the system's lack of commercial success and its performance problems (p. 186). That appears to satisfy I2. If I2 is accepted, the *Hardware–language trade-off* and *Adoption factors* fields above apply; they are filled but flagged.
- No exclusion criterion applies: X5 book chapter allowed; X6 English; X7 obtained. X9 does not apply: the chapter's title does not present it as a survey (though the book surveys capability systems), and it states its own reasons (p. 186). X8 cannot be judged from my inputs. Other 432 records (e.g. Organick 1983, Cox et al. 1983, cited p. 186) may overlap. This chapter adds a third-party fate assessment (p. 186).
- For SQ1 the useful pointer is Cox, Korwin, Lai and Pollack (1983), cited as discussing "the Intel 432 interprocess communication facility used for both message passing and process scheduling" (p. 186). The chapter itself does not say that scheduling uses messages. It may be worth checking whether Cox 1983 is in the review or should be snowballed.
