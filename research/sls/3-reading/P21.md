## First pass: the five Cs
- **Category:** Design and implementation description of a research prototype many-core processor chip, with silicon measurements (power, maximum frequency, two NAS benchmarks) (pp. 173, 179–180).
- **Context:** Follows Intel's 80-tile TeraFLOPS network-on-chip [1] (pp. 173, 175); message-passing protocol "inspired by software coherency models such as SHMEM [9], MPI and openMP [10]" (p. 176); the programmer's view is in Mattson et al. [11] (p. 176).
- **Correctness:** The motivating claim, that "the computational value gained through additional cores will at some point be exceeded by the protocol overhead needed to maintain cache coherency among the cores" (p. 173), is asserted without measurement or citation. The measured results (silicon, Fmax, power, NAS LU/BT speedup) support the claims about power and frequency range. The paper does not compare against a coherent design.
- **Contributions:** A 48-core, 24-tile 6×4 2D-mesh chip with no hardware cache coherency; per-tile 16 KB Message Passing Buffer (MPB, "on-die shared memory"); a new cache-line memory type (MPMT) and a new instruction (INVDMB) for software-managed coherency; a 4-stage virtual cut-through router; 4 DDR3 controllers; 8 voltage and 28 frequency islands for DVFS (pp. 173–178).
- **Clarity:** Clearly organised circuits paper; the message-passing protocol is given in one short section (pp. 176–177) and much of its semantics (naming, synchronisation, busy receivers) is left to the cited programmer's-view paper [11]. Fig. 7(b) and Fig. 11 are not labelled as measured or simulated in their captions.

## Notes
- **Source read:** full text, publisher's typeset version (IEEE JSSC vol. 46, no. 1, pp. 173–183), ResearchGate upload by Michael Konow (1 June 2014) with a cover sheet, obtained from the mirror https://search.iczhiku.com/paper/WjR2NH8uksdALAbZ.pdf and saved as `papers/P21.pdf` (12 PDF pages). Unpaywall and Semantic Scholar report the paper as closed access; the ResearchGate direct link returned 403; a Semantic Scholar PDF link found by search (pdfs.semanticscholar.org/a768/...) is an unrelated slide deck. Figures were read from rendered page images.
- **Criterion (for gate 3c):** I1 holds only in a qualified way. The paper names "message passing" throughout (title, p. 173) and adds hardware for it, the MPB, MPMT and INVDMB (pp. 174, 176–177), which fits I1(b) "a hardware or microcode mechanism". But the paper itself calls the protocol "a novel message passing based software protocol used to maintain data consistency in shared memory" (p. 173), and data moves by loads and stores into "on-die shared memory" (p. 174); there is no send instruction (p. 174). X2 does not apply as worded, because the instruction set and hardware do have message-specific mechanisms (MPB, MPMT, INVDMB, pp. 174, 177). The paper also plausibly meets I4 for the many-core scaling SQs served by the *Many-core scaling* field (SQ13, SQ14) and for *Model equivalence* (SQ10), since it builds message passing on non-coherent shared memory and argues against hardware coherency at scale (pp. 173, 176) (inferred; I did not read the SQ files). Verdict change, if any, is yours.
- **Hardware messages beyond core-to-core:** voltage change commands are messages to the VRC, a mesh agent "addressable by all cores" (p. 178); every on-die agent communicates by packets (p. 176). This is relevant to SQ1 at the hardware level even though the core-to-core path is shared-memory based.
- **Scope of results:** NAS benchmarks were run only up to 32 (LU) and 36 (BT) cores, not 48, at 533 MHz (p. 179); the paper says the benchmarks "were successfully ported to the processor architecture with minimal effort" (p. 179) without stating what the port involved.
- **Small inconsistencies:** the abstract gives nominal 1.1 V for 1 GHz cores / 2 GHz mesh (p. 173), while the 125 W typical point is at 1.14 V (pp. 180–181); core Fmax starts at 300 MHz at 730 mV (p. 179) while the conclusion and Fig. 14 give 0.7 V and 125 MHz as an operating point (pp. 180–181). The ResearchGate cover gives February 2011; the journal header gives January 2011 (p. 173).
- The programmer's model (synchronisation, receiver naming, what happens when a receiver is not ready) is deferred to Mattson et al. [11], "The Intel 48-core single-chip cloud computer (SCC) processor: Programmer's view" (p. 181); candidate for snowballing.
- **Criteria (gate 3c, 2026-10-12):** I1 and I4. *Model equivalence* is filled in the follow-up pass with the SQ files. I4 is met through SQ10 (message passing built as a software protocol on non-coherent on-die shared memory, with its latency against main memory, pp. 173, 176–177), SQ13 (a many-core processor without hardware cache coherency whose cores communicate by message passing, and how it is programmed, pp. 173, 176, 179; why it was or was not adopted is not stated) and SQ14 (limits named for many-core scaling: coherency protocol overhead, argued without measurement, and power density, p. 173; DDR3 I/O power at low power, p. 180). The paper does not directly answer SQ8, SQ9, SQ11, SQ12, SQ15–SQ19: it has a central VRC for voltage (p. 178) but does not argue that the function needs central coordination (SQ15), and the reconfigurable LUT (p. 175) is not presented as interposition (SQ17).
