# C12: Cost of one message send

## Definition
The time for one send and receive, or one IPC round trip, and what it is attributed to. Merges "RPC granularity / relative RPC overhead", "IPC fast path", "one-way message overhead (Ts, Tb)", "operating system overhead", "tail forwarding" and "small spaces".

## First found in
P46

## What the papers say, compared
- **Hardware message mechanisms: a few to about ten cycles, by their designers.** The 1987 MDP design handles a message in "less than ten clock cycles" against "about 300µs" on the Cosmic Cube, iPSC and S-NET (P69, pp. 190, 195–196); later papers give delivery plus dispatch "< 3µs" or "< 2 µs" against 5 ms on an iPSC (P64, p. 3; P12, p. 2), with iPSC configurations not given. The 1993 evaluation measures 11 cycles per message plus 0.5 cycles per byte, against 109–3200 cycles on the CM-5, nCUBE/2, DELTA and Active Messages (P65, p. 4). The M-Machine delivers a one-hop message in 5 cycles in simulation, with a 48-cycle software send path inside a remote read (P66, pp. 8–9).
- **Kernel IPC: from about 190 cycles to over 100 µs.** Mach IPC took 115 µs against 5 µs for L4 on a 486-DX50 (P46, pp. 73–75); seL4 reaches 188 cycles one way on ARM11 with a hand-optimised fast path (P48, pp. 135, 146). Barrelfish's URPC costs 450 cycles against 424 for L4 IPC on the same machine (P8, p. 10). A KeyKOS send costs "less than 500 cycles" on the 88x00 (P53, pp. 7, 13–16); an EROS round trip 2.38–3.21 µs on a 400 MHz Pentium II, where small address spaces have "a disproportionate impact" (P77, pp. 177–178, 180–183). Helios: about 10 µs locally (P45, p. 10).
- **Software layers over the hardware add most of the cost.** On the J-Machine a Concurrent Smalltalk call takes about 100 instructions (81–162 simulated), a tail-forwarded call about 36, and about 70% of active time is in the operating system (P70, pp. 104–108). Active Messages cut start-up from 160 to 30 µs on the nCUBE/2 by removing buffer management from the vendor library (P33, pp. 432–434).
- **Causes.** Liedtke puts "at least 73%" of the microkernel penalty on IPC, with hardware switching "only 3%–7%", and blames kernel structure and implementation (P46, pp. 74–75). Finer-grained objects and distribution will make calls between address spaces more frequent, so their cost matters more over time (P46, p. 73). The 432's procedure call, measured instead of its Send, costs 982 cycles, about 10 times a call on a 68010 or VAX (P10, pp. 308–310).
- **Comparison.** Hardware sends are one to two orders of magnitude cheaper than kernel IPC, but the J-Machine shows the gain can be lost in the software above it. Units (cycles or µs), clock rates and baselines differ across papers, and most figures are the designers' own.
