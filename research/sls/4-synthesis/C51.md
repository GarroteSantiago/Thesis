# C51: Network bandwidth as the scaling limit

## Definition
For machines of message-passing nodes, interconnect bandwidth and topology bound the number of nodes and how finely programs can communicate. Merges "terminal network bandwidth" and the network limits stated in the J-Machine papers.

## First found in
P65

## What the papers say, compared
- **Design.** The number of nodes is limited "by the addressability of the router and the bandwidth of the network", and a 3-D mesh is said to give "the lowest latency and highest throughput" (P64, pp. 2, 5).
- **Measured.** "global bandwidth is a critical resource that limits the computation to communication ratio"; under random traffic the network saturates at 6 of 14.4 Gbit/s bisection, and terminal bandwidth "often becomes the limiting factor" for neighbour communication (P65, pp. 2, 4–6, 11).
- **Projected.** The network "should not become a critical resource until a J-Machine with over 7³ = 343 nodes is built"; with random allocation on 65,536 nodes "the network will become hopelessly congested" unless locality is exploited (P70, pp. 110, 114, 121).
- **On a multiprocessor.** Barrelfish's NUMA-aware multicast stays near 3k cycles while broadcast and unicast grow linearly to about 13–14k at 32 cores (P8, p. 13).
- **Comparison.** The J-Machine papers agree the network is the limit and locality the cure; none of them shows how to get locality, which P70 leaves open.
