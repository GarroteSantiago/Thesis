# C3: General mechanisms, not a hardwired model or policy

## Definition
The machine or kernel provides a few primitive mechanisms from which several programming models or policies are built, instead of building one model or one policy in. Merges "primitive mechanisms vs programming models", "general mechanisms rather than a hardwired model", "single primitive message (EXECUTE)", "policy inside the microkernel / mechanism, not policy" and the "microkernel minimality principle".

## First found in
P12

## What the papers say, compared
- **Processor designers argue for general mechanisms.** The MDP gives general mechanisms for communication, synchronisation and naming instead of specialising for one model, and is claimed "competitive with processing nodes specialized to a single model of computation such as iWARP" and the Transputer (P12, pp. 1–2, 23); it supports actors, dataflow and communicating processes (P64, pp. 1–2, 13). Only one message, EXECUTE, is hard-wired; richer messages run "almost as efficiently" as ROM macrocode (P69, p. 191). These claims are argued, not measured.
- **A third party goes further.** One fast primitive is "more versatile than supporting a single paradigm with special hardware", and software handlers "can exploit and optimize special cases" (P33, pp. 431, 437).
- **Kernel designers say the same of policy.** The weakness of first-generation microkernels "is the policy inside the microkernel"; L4 "provides a pure mechanism interface" and leaves policies to user-level servers (P46, pp. 74–76). Twenty years on, the minimality principle is "Retained" (P48, p. 136).
- **Where the principle stops.** L4 kept a fixed-policy scheduler in the kernel, because "All past attempts to export scheduling policy to user level have failed, generally due to intolerable overheads" (P48, p. 138). So the papers agree on the principle but report at least one function where it has not held.
