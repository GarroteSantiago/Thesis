# C10: Asynchronous and split-phase communication

## Definition
The sender continues after sending; the reply or completion arrives later, signalled by a flag, a message or a stored continuation. Merges "split-phase operation" (two records), "pipelining and batching of messages", "overlap of communication and computation", "asynchronous endpoint / notification" and "stateless, transaction-oriented server with continuations".

## First found in
P8

## What the papers say, compared
- **Designs for many cores start asynchronous.** In the multikernel an operation sends a request and continues, with several requests in flight or batched in one message for throughput (P8, pp. 2, 6). fos sends asynchronously to named mailboxes, and its servers store a continuation for long operations instead of blocking or locking (P102, pp. 6–7).
- **On multicomputers it is the "real architectural issue".** Active Messages replace "latency tolerance" with overlap of communication and computation; Split-C's remote PUT and GET return at once and signal completion by a flag (P33, pp. 431, 434).
- **A synchronous kernel had to add it.** L4 added a single-word asynchronous notification, where a send ORs a bit mask into the word and the receiver blocks or polls (P48, pp. 136–137), because synchronous-only IPC forced multithreaded designs (P48, pp. 136–139).
- **Hardware futures are the same idea at the word level** (C5).
- **Comparison.** No paper read argues for synchronous messages across cores; the one kernel that began synchronous moved partly away from it (P48), which matches the multicore designs (P8, P102).
