# C13: Inherent versus implementation causes of performance

## Definition
Whether a measured slowness is attributed to the concept of the design or to how it was built (compiler, chip technology, code), and how that attribution is made. Merges "cycle sink", "synthetic 432", "inherent cost of object orientation", "artifacts vs concepts", "high-level language system = compiler + machine", "wait states", "normalized performance" and "time and space cost of the 432 approach".

## First found in
P10

## What the papers say, compared
- **Two third-party evaluations of the 432 isolate causes, and frame them differently.** Hansen et al. measure the 432 at about 1/20 of a VAX-11/780 and isolate the loss to parameter and global access in release 2 by modified programs (P29, pp. 18, 20, 23), but warn that "a high-level language system consists of the compiler and the machine, so we are not measuring just the architecture" (P29, p. 17). Colwell et al. separate the two: by simulator logs they attribute 25–35% to the Ada compiler and 5–10% to bit alignment and missing literals, build a "synthetic 432" with these fixed, and call the remaining 2–3x "our estimate for the inherent cost of the 432's style of object orientation" (P10, pp. 312–337). That inherent part is a residual, not isolated, and their summary percentages do not all match their tables (P10, pp. 329, 336–337). Their verdict: performance "dominated, in large part, by artifacts and not by concepts" (P10, p. 338).
- **Comparing machines is itself hard.** Wait states and normalisation to one clock rate change the ranking (P29, pp. 18–19, 22); cross-machine comparisons with different languages are a "rough estimate" (P10, pp. 309, 336).
- **Designers mostly attribute without isolating.** Liedtke blames the "basic architecture" and implementation of first-generation kernels, not microkernels as such (P46, pp. 73–75, 77). KeyKOS's designers call one cost a mistake of implementation ("we were mistaken") and another "a direct result of the underlying KeyKOS I/O design" (P53, pp. 13–16). The MDP's designers attribute a 3–4x area and 2–3x speed loss to standard cells (P12, p. 16). Barrelfish's authors call some overheads implementation (dispatch loop, threads library) and some design (avoiding kernel crossings) (P8, pp. 13, 15–16). Minix 3's call their slightly lower performance inherent to the design and also say the system "has not been optimized" (P49, pp. 47, 49–50).
- **A third party calls one cost inherent to message-driven hardware**: lack of locality, argued not measured (P33, p. 435).
- **Comparison.** Only the 432 has been through an independent cause-by-cause analysis; everywhere else the attribution is the designers' and unmeasured, which limits what can be said about whether a message machine's losses are avoidable.
