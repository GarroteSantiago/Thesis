# Coverage of SQ14

Saved as `sls/4-synthesis/SQ14.coverage.md`. Which papers serve this question, what each added, and whether the question has what it needs (`research/sls/4-synthesis/step.md`).

## Rule
- **Kind:** evidence.
- **Unit:** a finding: a measurement, projection or argument that hardware shared memory, cache coherence or a shared-memory kernel does or does not scale to many cores, or a limit reported on many-core scaling in general (power, communication, serial fractions).
- **Enough when:** at least 10 papers serving it are read, in the reading order, then 5 in a row add nothing new (10 + 5).

## Read
Named papers first (P22, P8, P102), then the others.
| # | Paper | Contributes | New? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | P22 | Coherence scales, analytically: traffic per miss independent of core count and at most 20% above no coherence; storage √C or ∛C (5% or 2% at 512 cores); recalls under 0.1%; indirect misses 1.5–2x; interconnect and software scalability left out (pp. 3–9) | new |
| 2 | P8 | Shared-state updates cost ~12,000 extra cycles each at 16 cores, "stalled on cache misses"; Linux and Windows unmap scale worse from serial IPIs; cites a coherence limit "to even 80 cores"; authors warn against quantitative conclusions (pp. 3–4, 13, 16) | new |
| 3 | P102 | Linux 2.6.24.7's page allocator "does not scale beyond 8 cores under heavy load" (16 cores); simulated OS/application cache interference; coherence scaling called "unclear" without a source (pp. 2–5, 8–9) | new |
| 4 | P21 | Argued by designers: coherence overhead will exceed the value of more cores; power density as a general limit; measured: memory controllers dominate power at low voltage because DDR3 I/O cannot scale (pp. 173, 180) | new |
| 5 | P45 | A shared-memory kernel on 4 cores: one dispatcher lock limits scaling, two kernels scale around it; IPIs for balancing "harm performance as the number of cores grows"; remote misses 38% slower (pp. 9, 11) | new |
| 6 | P64 | Design statement for a machine with no shared memory: node count limited by router addressability and network bandwidth; 3-D mesh chosen for latency and throughput (pp. 2, 5) | new |
| 7 | P65 | Measured: "global bandwidth is a critical resource"; the network saturates at 6 of 14.4 Gbit/s under random traffic (pp. 2, 4, 11) | new |
| 8 | P70 | Analytical: the network becomes critical above 343 nodes; random allocation would congest a 65,536-node machine; a name-space limit of about 32,000 objects per node (pp. 110, 114, 121, 123) | new |
