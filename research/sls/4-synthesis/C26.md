# C26: Language-based protection

## Definition
Isolation between processes enforced by a type-safe language and its compiler or verifier instead of hardware address spaces. Merges "software isolation (from Singularity)" and "language-based protection".

## First found in
P45

## What the papers say, compared
- **Singularity and its descendant.** Singularity protects by language safety and verification rather than the MMU, as the Burroughs B5000 did (P49, p. 49). Helios inherits this: processes are isolated by type and memory safety, all in one address space at ring 0, so no MMU is required (P45, pp. 4, 6), and remote channels keep isolation by type safety, not hardware (P45, pp. 4, 6–7, 10).
- **The opposite choice in a language machine.** The Lisp Machine deliberately makes "no attempt to have the language automatically forbid constructs that circumvent the contract", so internal state can be inspected from a debugger (P73, p. 282).
- **Comparison.** Both language-protected systems read rest on a trusted part outside the language (C42); no paper read measures the cost of language protection against hardware protection.
