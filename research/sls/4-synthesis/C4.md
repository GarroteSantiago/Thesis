# C4: Fine-grain concurrency and grain size

## Definition
Programs split into many short tasks on many small, cheap nodes; the grain size is the average work a task does before it suspends. Merges "fine-grain multicomputer / jellybean node", "fine-grain concurrent computer (grain size)", "physically fine-grain machines", "fine-grained communication" and "grain size".

## First found in
P12

## What the papers say, compared
- **The design target was very small.** The 1987 MDP aims at about 10 instructions per task, against a "natural grain" of about 20 instruction times and several hundred on existing machines (P69, pp. 190, 195). The 1989 and 1992 papers keep tasks of about 20 instructions, objects of about 8 words and nodes with about 10^4 bytes, as cheap "jellybean" parts (P64, pp. 2, 13; P12, pp. 1–2, 23).
- **Measurement found a larger grain.** On the simulated J-Machine the measured grain is about 60 instructions, which the author found "surprising; it was expected to be much lower" (P70, abstract, p. 7). The 1993 evaluation describes threads of 50 to 150 instructions (P65, p. 1).
- **The economic argument is the designers'.** Physically fine-grain machines give "proportionally better cost/performance while retaining the same total memory capacity" (P65, p. 12), and small messages are cheap enough that data need not be sent in large blocks (P65, pp. 5, 9).
- **A third party names a cost.** Short contexts leave no locality to exploit, which limits processing performance (P33, p. 435).
