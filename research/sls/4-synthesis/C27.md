# C27: Ownership transfer of message data

## Definition
A message's payload passes from sender to receiver so that only one party can reach it at a time, which avoids copying without creating shared state. Merges "single-owner shared heap", "channel contract and exchange heap" (ownership part) and "data race through a message-passed reference".

## First found in
P9

## What the papers say, compared
- **Enforced by the language.** In Singularity "at each instant every object on the heap belongs to a single process. However, ownership of an object can be passed over a channel", allowing zero-copy I/O (P49, p. 50). Helios keeps message data in an exchange heap whose ownership is checked at compile time (P45, p. 5); between kernels it copies once (P45, pp. 6–7).
- **Not enforced: races follow.** In Go, sending a reference to a shared variable over a channel lets both goroutines reach it, and real bugs result (Docker#22985, CockroachDB#6111) (P9, p. 9).
- **Copying instead.** Barrelfish copies cache-line-sized messages through URPC channels (P8, p. 9).
- **Comparison.** The only empirical evidence (P9) shows what happens without enforcement; no paper read measures defects or costs with enforced ownership transfer.
