# C47: Kinds of concurrency defect

## Definition
Taxonomies of real concurrency bugs by pattern and by behaviour. Merges "atomicity violation", "order violation", "manifestation condition", "multi-variable concurrency bug", "data race (not a bug pattern)", "deadlock", "order intention", "blocking bug", "non-blocking bug" and "Go's new implementation of existing primitives".

## First found in
P5

## What the papers say, compared
- **By pattern (C/C++, shared variables and locks).** Of 105 bugs in four applications, 74 are non-deadlock (51 atomicity violations, 24 order violations, 2 other, 3 counted twice) and 31 deadlocks; 25 of 74 involve several variables; 101 of 105 need at most two threads (P5, pp. 331–336). Data races are left out as a category because "data-race free does not mean concurrency bug free" (P5, pp. 329, 332). Order intentions "cannot be easily enforced by the basic TM design or locks" (P5, p. 338).
- **By behaviour and cause (Go).** Of 171 bugs in six applications, 85 are blocking, a category broader than deadlock, and 86 non-blocking; 105 come from shared memory and 66 from message passing (P9, p. 5). Go's own versions of familiar primitives (RWMutex giving writers priority) cause new bugs (P9, p. 6).
- **Comparison.** P5 classifies by what went wrong in memory, P9 by how goroutines communicate; P9's "blocking" includes waits that P5 would not call deadlock. Different languages and populations prevent direct comparison of counts.
