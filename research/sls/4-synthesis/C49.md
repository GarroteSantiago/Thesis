# C49: Scalability of cache coherence

## Definition
Whether the cost of keeping caches coherent grows slowly enough with the number of cores. Merges "single-writer, multiple-reader invariant", "scalable coherence", "amortized coherence traffic", "exact vs inexact encoding of sharers", "hierarchical inclusive coherence", "explicit eviction notifications", "recall (back-invalidation)", "direct vs indirect miss" and "complexity shifting".

## First found in
P22

## What the papers say, compared
- **It scales, analytically.** With exact sharer tracking, coherence traffic per miss does not depend on core count and is at most 20% above caches without coherence; hierarchical storage grows as the square or cube root of core count (5% or 2% of cache at 512 cores); recalls stay below 0.1% of misses; indirect misses cost 1.5–2 times a non-coherent miss (P22, pp. 3–7). These are analytical results on one chip, with no queuing modelled, and interconnect and "software non-scalability" left out (P22, pp. 3–9). Removing coherence "shifts the complexity from hardware to software" (P22, p. 9).
- **It may not, by argument.** The SCC's designers say "the computational value gained through additional cores will at some point be exceeded by the protocol overhead" (P21, p. 173). fos says "It is also unclear whether cache coherent shared memory will scale to large core counts", without a cited source (P102, pp. 2–5). Barrelfish cites a limit "to even 80 cores" (P8, p. 4).
- **Measured costs are at kernel and lock level.** Updating shared state costs about 12,000 extra cycles per update at 16 cores, "stalled on cache misses" (P8, p. 3); Linux and Windows unmap scale worse from serial IPIs (P8, p. 13); Linux's page allocator "does not scale beyond 8 cores under heavy load" (P102, pp. 3–4, 8–9); Helios's single dispatcher lock limits scaling on 4 cores (P45, pp. 9, 11).
- **Comparison.** The pro side is analysis of the protocol; the con side is argument plus measurements of software bottlenecks, which P22 explicitly sets outside coherence. The two sides do not yet meet on the same evidence (P23, P24, P98 unread).
